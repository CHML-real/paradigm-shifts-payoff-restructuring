## Abstract

This paper develops and empirically tests a game-theoretic account of scientific paradigm shifts, arguing that the timing and character of paradigm adoption in scientific communities is governed not by evidential accumulation alone but by shifts in the payoff structure that individual scientists face. The central claim is that cognitive leaps acquire social meaning through the game-theoretic framework of the communities in which they occur: what counts as a meaningful departure from normal inference is a function of the payoff matrix of the relevant evaluative game, not of the intrinsic content of the departure.

Drawing on Kuhn's account of scientific revolutions, the study integrates three empirical anchors:
- **Azoulay et al. (2019):** non-collaborator publication flows increase **8.6%** following star scientist death; top-cited contributions surge **25.3%**
- **Shwed & Bearman (2010):** consensus formation operationalized through citation network modularity — revealing spiral, cyclical, and flat trajectories
- **Rodriguez-Sickert et al. (2015):** agent-based model showing non-linear paradigm migration from conservative/revolutionary force interaction

Applied to **seven historical paradigm shifts** across three centuries and six scientific disciplines.

## Core Argument

```
Cognitive leap occurs        →  Physically or cognitively generated departure from normal inference
Community evaluates          →  Game-theoretic payoff structure determines reception
Payoff structure shifts      →  What was noise becomes signal; what was insight becomes orthodoxy
Meaning is retrospective     →  Significance is not intrinsic — it is a function of the game
```

**Three principal findings:**

1. **Payoff restructuring is a consistent correlate** of paradigm transitions — in 6 of 7 cases, payoff shifts preceded or co-occurred with paradigm transition
2. **Payoff restructuring is necessary but not sufficient** — consistent with Marx & Bornmann's (2013) Anna Karenina principle: multiple conditions must be simultaneously fulfilled
3. **Planck's Principle is more complex than assumed** — age is a minor predictor; payoff position within the field is the more reliable determinant

## Key Claims

**On payoff structure and paradigm shifts:**
> Payoff restructuring is a consistent correlate of paradigm transitions. In six of seven cases, measurable shifts in the payoff structure preceded or co-occurred with the onset of paradigm transition, with the timing of individual defection correlating more closely with payoff position than with evidential exposure.

**On the Anna Karenina principle:**
> Payoff restructuring is necessary but not sufficient. Multiple necessary conditions — evidential convergence, methodological innovation, and institutional reconfiguration — must be simultaneously fulfilled for a paradigm shift to succeed. The failure of any single condition is sufficient to forestall transition.

**On Planck's Principle:**
> Age is a minor and inconsistent predictor of adoption timing. What mattered in the plate tectonics case was not generational cohort, but the position of individual scientists within the payoff structure of the discipline — consistent with the game-theoretic prediction.

**On the AI novelty connection:**
> This study provides empirical support for the game-theoretic component of the parent paper's account of meaning-making. However, it explicitly acknowledges the gap between the *evaluative* dimension addressed here and the *generative* dimension addressed in the parent paper. A complete account requires additional evidence on the relationship between neural stochasticity and the generation — not merely the evaluation — of novel cognitive content.

## Disconfirming Cases

Four disconfirming cases are explicitly included and analyzed:

- **National Cancer Act (1971):** Strong payoff shift without paradigm shift → confirms Anna Karenina principle
- **Lab-level germ theory adoption:** Local paradigm shift without broad payoff restructuring → consistent with game-theoretic account under low institutional commitment conditions
- **Plate tectonics older adopters:** Older geologists adopted earlier than Planck predicts → confirms payoff position over age
- **Tobacco-cancer cyclical trajectory:** Deliberate payoff manipulation disrupted consensus without changing evidence → confirms mechanism; shows bounds set by evidence

## Relation to Parent Paper

This meta-analysis was undertaken as a companion to:

> lajjadred (2026). *Beyond Deductive Machinery: Why LLMs Cannot Generate Truly Novel Conclusions.* Zenodo. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20324204

The parent paper argues that LLMs are structurally incapable of generating genuinely novel conclusions because they lack:
1. Physical indeterminacy at the hardware level
2. The capacity for logical leaps grounded in biological stochasticity
3. The capacity for existential prior-setting

The present study provides empirical support for **claim (3)'s evaluative dimension**: the determination of which cognitive leaps are meaningful is governed by game-theoretic payoff structures, not by intrinsic content. This is consistent with the parent paper's account but does not establish the generative claims independently.
